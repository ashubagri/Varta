13. Kuṇḍalinī and the nāḍīs

Several Tantric and Haṭha Yoga traditions describe Kuṇḍalinī as a latent spiritual power, often symbolised as coiled near the base of the subtle body. Nāḍīs are channels through which subtle vitality is said to move. Three frequently emphasised channels are Iḍā, Piṅgalā and Suṣumṇā. Their descriptions vary among texts and lineages.

Traditional sources sometimes speak of 72,000 nāḍīs or other large numbers. These figures belong to subtle-body cosmology; they are not a count of nerves, blood vessels or lymphatic channels. Modern anatomy has not identified Iḍā, Piṅgalā or Suṣumṇā as physical tubes. Their contemplative significance does not depend on claiming that they are anatomical structures.

The nasal cycle is a real physiological phenomenon: airflow resistance commonly alternates between nostrils over time through autonomic regulation of nasal tissues. This resemblance may invite comparison with Iḍā and Piṅgalā symbolism, but resemblance is not proof that subtle channels are physical anatomy or that nostril dominance reliably switches brain hemispheres.

Safety: Do not force breath retention, prolonged inhalation or nostril closure. Stop if dizzy, wheezy, breathless or distressed. People with respiratory illness should keep any practice gentle and follow medical advice.

14. Sūrya Bhedana and Candra Bhedana

Traditional pranayama manuals describe Sūrya Bhedana (solar-piercing breath) and Candra Bhedana (lunar-piercing breath). In some systems, the right nostril is associated with solar qualities and the left with lunar qualities. These are symbolic and practice-based associations, not universal physiological laws.

Studies have explored whether unilateral nostril breathing changes autonomic measures or attention. Methods and results vary, and the evidence does not justify claims that a nostril reliably switches a cerebral hemisphere, precisely controls body temperature or activates a measurable energy channel. The nasal cycle is real; the traditional interpretation is a distinct layer.

If a gentle alternate-nostril practice is comfortable, breathe naturally without holding or forcing airflow. Avoid any technique that provokes breathlessness, wheezing or dizziness. The purpose of a contemplative practice should be steadiness, not a contest to manipulate physiology.

15. Pañca Mahābhūta: five great elements

Many Indian philosophical systems describe five great elements: Pṛthvī (earth), Āpas/Jala (water), Tejas/Agni (fire), Vāyu (air) and Ākāśa (space or ether). Their meanings vary by school. They are philosophical categories for interpreting qualities of experience, matter and the cosmos, not a list of chemical elements in the modern periodic table.

Some frameworks link the elements to sensory qualities: sound with ākāśa, touch with vāyu, form or colour with tejas, taste with water and smell with earth. These correspondences belong to particular cosmologies. They are not modern sensory neuroscience or a claim that sound physically travels through a substance called ākāśa.

Ākāśa should not simply be equated with the modern vacuum or spacetime. Physics distinguishes vacuum, spacetime, fields and matter using mathematical theories and experiments. Ākāśa has a history of philosophical meanings that differ among schools. Comparison may be useful as analogy, but “Vedic Ākāśa is quantum vacuum” is not a demonstrated identity.

The five-element model remains valuable to study as a historical classification and contemplative language for solidity, fluidity, heat, movement and spatial openness. Its significance does not depend on treating it as modern chemistry.

16. Frequency, resonance and quantum comparisons

Frequency describes a rate of repetition. It can apply to sound, electromagnetic radiation, circuits and many periodic processes. But a 100 Hz tone in air, a 100 Hz electromagnetic oscillation and a neural rhythm measured in EEG have different physical carriers, scales and mechanisms.

Quantum mechanics describes physical systems through mathematical states and observables. Quantum field theory uses fields as fundamental entities in its framework. These theories do not establish that thoughts, intentions or chakras are simply “quantum vibrations.” Popular spiritual writing often borrows quantum terms metaphorically; that does not make the borrowing a scientific explanation.

Resonance is a technical concept in physics and engineering. It is not a universal explanation for attraction, healing, consciousness or awakening. Ask what is oscillating, in what medium or field, how frequency is measured, what mechanism is proposed and what observation could disprove the claim. If these questions have no clear answers, present the phrase as metaphor or philosophy rather than science.
