From Body to Mind to Self: The Inner Journey of Indian Festivals

Navratri, Dussehra and Diwali can be contemplated as three connected stages of inner transformation: disciplining the body, purifying the mind and recognising the light of the Self.

An original interpretive framework presented by Ashu Bagri on Hydrogen Awakening. This article preserves the creator's central idea while distinguishing scriptural references, philosophical interpretation and modern scientific evidence.

The central idea

Festivals are not only occasions for external celebration. They can also become reminders of an inward journey. In this Hydrogen Awakening interpretation, the sequence moves from the visible and tangible to the subtle and inward:

BODY → MIND → SELF

Discipline the body. Transform the mind. Let the light of awareness become clear.

1. Navratri — discipline and care of the body

The nine nights of Navratri are connected symbolically with the body's nine gates: two eyes, two ears, two nostrils, the mouth, and the two lower openings. The practice of fasting, restraint, prayer and mindful attention can be understood as a call to care for the body and become more conscious of sensory habits.

Scriptural connection: Bhagavad Gita 5.13 describes the embodied being as residing in the “city of nine gates” (nava-dvāre pure). The verse is a philosophical description of embodied existence; it does not itself prescribe a Navratri ritual of cleaning nine openings.

2. Dussehra — transformation of the mind

Ravana's ten heads can be read as a metaphor for destructive tendencies that multiply within the mind. Commonly discussed examples include desire, anger, greed, attachment, pride, jealousy, delusion, selfishness and related forms of ego-centred behaviour. Different traditions give different lists; there is no single universally fixed ten-vices list.

The burning of Ravana becomes an inner reminder: identify harmful tendencies, take responsibility for them and cultivate discernment, courage, compassion and self-control. The victory of Rama over Ravana is also a story with rich theological, literary and regional meanings beyond this personal interpretation.

3. Diwali — illumination and recognition of the Self

When the body is cared for and the mind becomes clearer, Diwali's lamps can symbolise the light of awareness. Rama's return to Ayodhya offers a powerful image of light returning to the inner kingdom. The celebration invites reflection on knowledge, renewal, hope and the triumph of dharma.

Vedantic refinement: Rather than saying that the eternal Self is literally cleaned, it is more precise to say that ignorance, confusion and mental conditioning are addressed. In many Vedantic teachings, Atman is not a physically or morally dirty object; the work is to remove misunderstanding and recognise what is already true.

The wider festival sequence in the video

  • Dhanteras: renewal of health, vitality and care for life.
  • Naraka Chaturdashi: confronting inner darkness and harmful patterns.
  • Govardhan Puja: gratitude, humility and relationship with nature.
  • Bhai Dooj: affection, mutual care and relationship harmony; symbolic readings of masculine and feminine energies are interpretive, not a universal definition of the festival.
  • Chhath Puja: reverence for the Sun and the natural conditions that sustain life, expressed through a distinctive devotional tradition.

What is philosophy, and what is science?

This framework is a spiritual and philosophical interpretation of festivals. The nine-gated body is a phrase found in the Bhagavad Gita; the mapping of Navratri's nine nights to bodily discipline and Ravana's ten heads to ten mental vices is a symbolic teaching. These mappings should not be presented as experimentally established physiology or psychology.

Fasting, sleep, attention, emotional regulation and contemplative practices can be studied scientifically, but specific health benefits depend on the practice, the person and the evidence. Fasting is not appropriate for everyone. People with medical conditions, pregnancy, medication needs or a history of disordered eating should seek suitable medical advice rather than assume fasting is beneficial.

The value of this framework is that it gives a memorable structure for self-reflection while leaving room for careful study, diverse traditions and scientific scrutiny.

हिंदी में सार — शरीर से मन और आत्मबोध तक

नवरात्रि: शरीर और इन्द्रियों के प्रति सजगता, संयम और अनुशासन। शरीर के नौ द्वारों का प्रतीकात्मक स्मरण।

दशहरा: मन की नकारात्मक प्रवृत्तियों—जैसे क्रोध, लोभ, अहंकार, मोह और ईर्ष्या—पर विजय का संकल्प। रावण के दस सिरों को इन प्रवृत्तियों का प्रतीक मानना एक आध्यात्मिक व्याख्या है।

दीपावली: अज्ञान और भ्रम के अंधकार से ज्ञान और जागरूकता के प्रकाश की ओर यात्रा। वेदान्त की दृष्टि से आत्मा को साफ करने के बजाय आत्मा के वास्तविक स्वरूप को पहचानना अधिक सटीक अभिव्यक्ति है।

मुख्य संदेश: शरीर में अनुशासन, मन में शुद्धता और आत्मबोध में प्रकाश — यही इस विचार-यात्रा का सार है।
