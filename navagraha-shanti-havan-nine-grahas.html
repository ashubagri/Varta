Ritual guide 04 · Jyotiṣa and worship

Navagraha Śānti Havan

Nine grahas, symbolic order and the practice of śānti.

Navagraha worship is found in several Hindu ritual and temple settings. The nine grahas are understood within Jyotiṣa and religious cosmologies; they are not simply a list of nine physical planets in modern astronomy. This guide separates traditional symbolism from astronomical description.

← Main fire-ritual guide

1. Who are the nine grahas?

  • Sūrya — Sun
  • Candra — Moon
  • Maṅgala — Mars
  • Budha — Mercury
  • Bṛhaspati or Guru — Jupiter
  • Śukra — Venus
  • Śani — Saturn
  • Rāhu — ascending lunar node in Jyotiṣa
  • Ketu — descending lunar node in Jyotiṣa

Rāhu and Ketu are mathematical points where the Moon's orbit intersects the ecliptic, not physical planets. The traditional category graha is broader than the modern astronomical category “planet”.

2. What does śānti mean here?

Śānti means peace, pacification or tranquillity. A Navagraha Śānti rite is undertaken within a religious framework to seek harmony, protection or relief from perceived planetary afflictions. This describes the ritual's traditional intention; it does not demonstrate that planetary positions cause a person's illness, financial outcomes or life events.

Academic research traces the development of planetary deities and later śānti rites through several textual and historical layers. Their forms were not fixed in one timeless package.

3. What may a havan include?

Depending on the paddhati and region, a ceremony may include saṅkalpa, Gaṇeśa worship, kalasha preparation, invocation of the grahas, offerings, recitation, homa and concluding prayers. The number and order of offerings, substances, colours, directions and mantras vary.

Important: There is no single universally authoritative nine-mantra sequence suitable for every Navagraha rite. The officiant should identify the ritual manual and tradition being followed. VARTA does not prescribe gemstone, fasting or remedial purchases based on an astrological claim.

4. Jyotiṣa and astronomy: related history, different methods

Indian astronomy developed sophisticated mathematical models for calendars, planetary motions and eclipses. Jyotiṣa also includes interpretive and predictive traditions. Modern astronomy tests physical models through observation, mathematics and reproducible measurement; astrological interpretations are cultural and divinatory frameworks and should not be presented as established causal science.

For example, an eclipse can be predicted astronomically through orbital geometry. The traditional story or ritual meaning attached to Rāhu and Ketu is a different kind of explanation.

5. Responsible participation

  • Ask for the name of the paddhati or textual source.
  • Understand the total cost and avoid pressure to buy costly objects or remedies.
  • Do not delay medical, legal or financial help because of an astrological interpretation.
  • Use safe ventilation and fire precautions during homa.
Health boundary: A ritual may be meaningful as devotion or community practice, but it is not a substitute for medical diagnosis or treatment.
