World Affairs

Understand international events through context, evidence, competing interests and reliable sources.

World affairs are shaped by states, businesses, institutions, technology, geography and people. Good analysis separates confirmed facts from claims, identifies whose perspective is represented, and recognises what remains uncertain.

Guide 01 · Energy & security

Energy security and geopolitics

Oil, gas, electricity, critical minerals and shipping routes influence national security and diplomacy. Energy transitions can create new dependencies as well as reduce old ones, so analysis should consider supply diversity, infrastructure, costs and resilience.

Guide 02 · Media literacy

How to read international news critically

Compare reporting from multiple credible outlets, look for primary documents and distinguish confirmed events from commentary. Check publication dates, quoted evidence, missing context and whether headlines overstate what the article actually establishes.

Guide 03 · Global economy

Trade, supply chains and strategic materials

Modern economies depend on cross-border networks for components, food, medicines, energy and minerals. Disruptions can spread through prices and production, while diversification often involves trade-offs between cost, resilience and environmental impact.

Guide 04 · Climate policy

Climate diplomacy and international cooperation

Climate agreements depend on national commitments, finance, technology, reporting and implementation. Countries have different historical emissions, capabilities and development needs, which makes negotiation complex and progress uneven.
