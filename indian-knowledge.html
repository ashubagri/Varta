Indian Knowledge

Explore Indian philosophy, Sanskrit, knowledge traditions and the history of ideas.

Indian knowledge traditions are diverse and span many centuries, languages, schools and communities. Read primary texts where possible, understand their historical setting, and distinguish traditional interpretation from modern scientific evidence.

Guide 01 · Texts & traditions

The Vedas and their place in Indian traditions

The four Vedas—Rigveda, Samaveda, Yajurveda and Atharvaveda—contain distinct collections and traditions. Their transmission involved careful oral practices. A useful study asks what a text says, how it was transmitted, and how different schools interpreted it.

Guide 02 · Language & logic

Sanskrit grammar and Pāṇini

Pāṇini's Aṣṭādhyāyī is a highly systematic work of Sanskrit grammar. Its concise rules and technical conventions have inspired comparisons with formal systems, but such comparisons should be made precisely rather than claiming that ancient grammar is identical to modern computing.

Guide 03 · Philosophy

The Upanishads: questions about self and reality

The Upanishads are a varied body of philosophical texts associated with the Vedic tradition. They explore questions about the self, ultimate reality, knowledge and liberation. Their ideas are interpreted differently across Indian philosophical schools.

Guide 04 · Memory traditions

Oral transmission and memory

Oral traditions can preserve complex material through repetition, recitation, teacher-student practice and structured learning. These methods deserve study on their own terms, while claims about their accuracy or cognitive effects should be evaluated using historical and empirical evidence.
