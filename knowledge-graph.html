Knowledge is interconnected, but connections are not proof of equivalence. A philosophical concept may inspire a scientific question without being a scientific result. A measurable physical process may illuminate a metaphor without proving the metaphor literally true. This graph is designed to make those relationships easier to explore and evaluate.

How the connections work

Science: claims should be testable where possible and supported by observations, methods, data and reproducible research.

Texts and history: claims should be grounded in identifiable texts, translations, dates, historical context and scholarly interpretation.

Philosophy and spirituality: concepts such as Śiva–Śakti, Nāda, Prāṇa and consciousness should be presented as philosophical, experiential or traditional frameworks unless a specific empirical claim has independent evidence.

Bridge questions: comparisons can be fruitful, but similarity of language or metaphor does not establish scientific identity or causation.
